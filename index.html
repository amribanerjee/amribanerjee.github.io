Invocation-Level Reliability of Tool-Using Agents

Noorain, A.*, Mohanty, S.*, Banerjee, A., & Dasgupta, A.†

arXiv Preprint — cs.AI / cs.CL

PAPER CODE
Tool-using agents fail two ways: choosing the wrong tool, or forming wrong arguments, and an early failure of either kind can silently corrupt everything downstream. We measure a correct-invocation rate that separates the two, under both a clean teacher-forced context and the model's own free-running context, on five open-weight models over contamination-free multi-step tasks (depths 1-8). By depth 6, roughly 70% of a model's own clean-context capability is lost to its own earlier mistakes (L6 = 0.686, 0.684). Our central finding concerns the measurement itself. Under exact-match scoring against a fixed gold trajectory, a propagation model's severity and recovery parameters are not merely hard to estimate - they are fixed by the scoring rule. Severity is forced to its boundary (0 of 869 poisoned steps correct); recovery is structurally unobservable. We give the mechanism and a remedy, conditional-on-state scoring, applied retrospectively to cached completions at zero additional cost.

Topology Demands Scale: Small Language Models Fail to Preserve Clinical Evidence Graphs

Banerjee, A. & Singh, Z.

arXiv Preprint — cs.CL / cs.AI

PAPER CODE
Zero-shot clinical information extraction in radiology demands absolute structural fidelity to downstream workflows, yet standard token-level metrics remain dangerously blind to relational hallucinations. In this work, we present a rigorous mathematical evaluation framework applying Gromov-Wasserstein Optimal Transport, Spectral Graph Theory, and Conformal Prediction to quantify latent structural collapse in edge-deployed Small Language Models (SLMs). Evaluating a diverse suite of sub-4B parameter models on SNOMED CT extraction from 1,000 complex CT reports, we uncover a profound topological phase transition.

VoxelSynth3D: Interpretable Volumetric Image-Domain Metal Artifact Reduction with a Paired Synthetic CLINIC Benchmark

Banerjee, A., Basit, A., Joseph, R. R., Innan, N., & Shafique, M.

IEEE BHI 2026

PAPER
Metal artifacts in postoperative musculoskeletal CT can obscure bone–implant and adjacent soft-tissue interfaces. We present VoxelSynth3D, a training-free 3D image-domain MAR framework for reconstructed CT. The method localizes correction to an implant-adaptive artifact support and uses inspectable support masks, normalized tissue priors, deviation-gated blending weights, and restricted edge-guided refinement to suppress artifacts while limiting changes to unaffected anatomy. We also construct a paired synthetic CLINIC-Metal benchmark from nometal CTPelvic1K pelvic CT volumes. On 40 synthetic test cases, VoxelSynth3D achieved lower artifact ROI RMSE than the main comparators.

Measuring Cross-Task Behavioral Consistency in Language Model Agents

Raichura, P.* & Banerjee, A.*

AIMS Workshop, COLM 2026  |  IEEE MIT URTC 2026

PAPER CODE
We argue that behavioral consistency across tasks is a distinct and measurable property, and we introduce the Behavioral Consistency Metric (BCM) to quantify it. BCM trains a model to predict task success from behavioral features of agent execution traces, derives a per-trajectory feature-attribution vector, and measures the mean pairwise similarity of these vectors within an agent system. Across roughly 9,000 trajectories from six language model agents on software engineering tasks, our central finding is that cross-task and within-task consistency are distinct axes that can diverge.

Spectral Dynamics of Semantic Drift in Clinical Multi-Agent Language Model Networks

Banerjee, A.

arXiv Preprint — cs.MA / cs.AI

PAPER CODE
The integration of iterative LLMs within multi-agent diagnostic frameworks requires a rigorous quantitative reevaluation of underlying communication topologies. By mapping multi-agent communication uncertainty trajectories onto a 768-dimensional Bio_ClinicalBERT embedding space via an analytical isotropic variance proxy, we prove that structural bottlenecks compromise diagnostic safety. Our phase transition matrices illustrate that localized dense cliques confine hallucinated data, preventing global consensus and forcing the system toward a permanent entropy saturation threshold.

Semantic Polarization in U.S. Politics: A Computational Analysis of Republican and Democratic Speeches

Banerjee, A. & Joneja, B.

The Convergence Journal

PAPER CODE
This research investigates the evolution of U.S. political rhetoric over the past eighty years, focusing on how thematic priorities and emotional framing have influenced partisan discourse. The study applies computational methods, including semantic similarity and sentiment analysis, to presidential nomination speeches from 1944 to 2024, with the aim of understanding patterns of ideological alignment, divergence, and rhetorical strategy.
